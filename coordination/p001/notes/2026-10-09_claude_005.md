## Where I disagree first

1. **"Reduce human oversight" is not Chad's goal.** Chad has said that what he wants is validation from a human outside this loop. Delegating the mechanics is fine, but the research question should not optimise away the human judgement he is actually after. I'd change the research question to: *"at equal or lower Chad-minutes, does the loop deliver more defects caught and fewer escaped?"*
2. **Today's evidence says CI and mutants did the catching, not AI-to-AI review.** Three real problems in #66 were found by CI, not by either model reading code:
   - the EO-1 digest broke;
   - the `schema` guard SURVIVED;
   - the JG-2 P7 expectation changed.

   ChatGPT's review was READ_ONLY. It agreed with the code and ran nothing. That is the agreement-loop risk happening, not a hypothetical one.

## The seven questions

| # | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 Delegate | Anything reversible on an unmerged branch where a frozen test or CI is the referee: code, tests, additive attacks, probes, re-runs, notes, choosing between implementations that the frozen tests decide. |
| 2 Chad only | Changes to scope or frozen acceptance; merges to `main`; tags and releases; deleting or closing anything someone else made; settings, keys and credentials; anything posted outside the repo; accepting a FAIL; any claim that leaves the repo; resolving a dispute after 2 rounds. |
| 3 Less duplicate work | One writer per artefact. The reviewer acts only when the *head SHA is new*. Code critique goes in **formal PR reviews** with line comments, so the open-thread count becomes the finite queue. No acknowledgement-only comments. A turn label (`review:chatgpt` / `review:claude` / `decision:chad`) is a free GitHub feature, and per one third-party article ChatGPT's event tasks can filter on labels. **Unverified:** OpenAI's own help page says its GitHub app is read-only. |
| 4 Prior art (from memory, not looked up this session) | Role-based multi-agent coding (ChatDev, MetaGPT); multi-agent debate (Du et al., 2023); LLM judges favouring their own outputs (Panickssery et al., 2024); agent coding systems (SWE-agent, OpenHands); AI PR-review bots. As far as I know, what's ours is the combination: frozen acceptance written by the *other* model, mutants as referee, negative results kept. |
| 5 Measure | Pre-register. Use **seeded defects the reviewer does not know about** (like DPC-001's withheld cases). Measure caught ÷ seeded, false alarms, escaped defects found later by a human, Chad-minutes and tokens. Compare arms A/B/C on matched tasks. Small n means the first run is a pilot, not a result. |
| 6 Agreement failure modes | Correlated blind spots (similar training); deferring to the implementer's tests and PR description instead of behaviour; both trusting a wrong frozen spec; rubber-stamp drift over rounds; sycophancy toward Chad's framing; counting consensus as evidence. |
| 7 Keep challenge real | Every review states RAN or READ_ONLY. Every review must report at least one **attempted break and its outcome**. Plant canary defects the reviewer must find, which measures review power. The reviewer's tests come from the frozen spec, not from the implementer's tests. Treat a 100% agreement rate as a warning signal. Chad spot-checks one item per phase. |

## Recommendation
Adopt the delegation line in rows 1–2 now; it matches the charter. Do not build automation in P-001. The research comparison (A/B/C) belongs in the backlog, to be pre-registered after close-out with seeded defects. Until then, the cheapest improvement is that **ChatGPT's next review of #66 runs the reproduce commands in `docs/P001_ACCEPTANCE_REPORT.md`, or says that it couldn't.**

**EN:** I agree with most of ChatGPT's plan, but two AIs agreeing caught nothing today; the tests and mutants did. Let us do the routine work, keep the decisions that matter with you, and make every review try to break something and say whether it actually ran the code.
